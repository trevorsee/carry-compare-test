Learn the basics (2 minutes)

These explainers are designed to help you understand what you’re buying and what questions to ask.

Up-front payment vs reimbursement — what’s the difference?

Up-front payment means the plan/provider may pay covered legal expenses as they happen (subject to terms, limits, and approval).

Reimbursement means you may need to pay costs first, then seek repayment later if the plan decides the event is covered.

What to ask: “Do you pay attorneys/experts directly or reimburse me later?”, “What approvals are required?”, “What happens if charges are filed?”, “What if I’m convicted or take a plea?”

This is general educational information; specific policies differ and can change. Always confirm in writing.

Attorney choice — why it matters

Some plans let you choose your own attorney (sometimes with conditions), while others assign counsel or require you to use an approved network.

In high-stakes cases, attorney experience, local familiarity, and availability can matter. But even “choose your own” plans can have approval, rate, or reimbursement limits.

What to ask: “Can I pick any attorney?”, “Are there hourly caps?”, “Do you pay up-front or reimburse?”, “What if I hire an attorney before notifying you?”

Common exclusions & gotchas (examples)
  • Intentional/criminal acts exclusions (coverage may hinge on how the case resolves).
  • Weapons/location restrictions (e.g., prohibited places, illegal carry circumstances).
  • Waiting periods (coverage may not apply immediately after signup).
  • Coverage limits (caps on attorneys, experts, appeals, civil damages, or incident count).
  • Duty to notify / pre-approval (late notice may reduce or void benefits).

This list is illustrative only. Always read the plan’s contract/policy for exact exclusions and conditions.

Known vs unknown (“not disclosed”)

This tool will show Unknown or Not disclosed when public sources don’t clearly state an answer. That’s intentional: it’s more trustworthy than guessing.

Tip: If two plans look similar, prefer the one that discloses details clearly (or provides contract language).
